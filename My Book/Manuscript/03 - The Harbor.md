Old Harbor smelled of tar and fish and other people's secrets. Mara walked the length of the quay with her arm in its sling and her collar up, and let the fog hide her.

Hollis the pawnbroker was gone. His shop had a new lock on the door and a sign that said CLOSED in a hand that had never been his. A woman selling eels told her the shop had changed hands a week ago. To whom, she would not say.

So Mara went to the one man on the harbor who would tell her the truth.

The harbormaster's office was a narrow room above the customs shed. Elias Crane sat at a high desk under a lamp, writing in a ledger of his own. He had grown a beard since she last saw him, and it had come in grey.

"Winston," he said, without looking up. "I heard you'd been shot."

"News travels."

"Down here it swims." He set down his pen. "Sit before you fall over."

She sat. For a moment neither of them spoke. They had shared a desk for six years once, and a dismissal, and a long silence after it. Some of that silence was still in the room.

"I'm looking for a black book," she said. "Pawned at Hollis's."

Elias went to a cabinet and came back with a slip of yellow paper. A pawn ticket. He laid it on the desk between them.

"Hollis left in a hurry. The new owner keeps his stock. And his records." He tapped the name on the ticket. "Your client pawned it himself. Eleven days ago. Long before any burglary."

Mara read the name twice. *A. Vane.* The handwriting was the same hand that had signed her fee.

"He lied," she said.

"Men like that don't lie, Mara. They arrange the truth." Elias leaned back. "There's more. The man who bought out Hollis is Captain Rook. He runs the night boats. If the book's still on the harbor, Rook has it."

"And if I want it back?"

"Then you'll need a friend who knows the tides." He reached for his coat. "Lucky for you, I've been bored for three years."

Outside, the fog was lifting. Somewhere up on the hill, in a house with too many lamps, Arthur Vane was waiting for her report. Mara decided he could wait a little longer.
