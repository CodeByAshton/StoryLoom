Mara waited until the house was asleep before she went back to the study.

She did not light the lamps. A detective learned early that light was a promise you made to whoever was watching. She worked by the grey glow from the window, running her fingertips over the desk the way a tailor reads cloth.

The blotter gave her something. Pressed into the green felt, faint and backwards, was the oval of a stamp. She tilted it toward the window and read it in reverse: *J. Hollis, Brokers. Old Harbor.*

So the ledger had not been stolen. It had been pawned.

She heard the floorboard a second too late.

The figure in the doorway was only a shape, a long coat and a raised arm. The flash lit the room white. Something struck her left shoulder like a thrown brick and turned her half around. She was on the carpet before the sound finished.

Footsteps on the stairs. A door. The garden gate, banging in the wind.

Mara lay among the scattered books and took inventory. Shoulder: bad. Head: clear. Pride: considerable damage. She pressed her palm against the wound and found it wet and warm and very much hers.

Lamplight swung into the hall. Arthur appeared in the doorway in his shirtsleeves with a lamp in one hand and a poker in the other.

"Detective! Good God."

"You're late, Vane."

"I heard the shot. I came at once." He knelt beside her, and his hands were shaking. "Did you see him?"

"I saw a coat."

"Then he'll have gone to the harbor. Thieves always do. Ships, you see. Easy to vanish." He said it quickly, as if he had practiced. "I'll send for a doctor. And the constables."

"No constables."

"You have been shot in my house. There will be constables."

She let him tie his handkerchief around her shoulder. While he worked, she watched his face in the lamplight and thought about the stamp in the blotter, and about how a man who kept the only key could still be robbed.

By dawn there were two constables at the gate of Blackwood Manor, and Mara had a doctor's stitches, a sling, and a destination.
